Our projects

The Council carries out many projects aimed at improving the lives of the villagers

Fighting home demolitions and discriminatory policy

About 360,000 Bedouin live in the Negev, about 40% of its residents. About 150,000 of them live in unrecognized villages or in conditions of an unrecognized village. All the unrecognized villages together sit on less than one percent of the Negev's land.

Read more ↓

These villages lack basic services: electricity, water, health and education. No house can get a building permit, so every house is under threat of demolition. Since the 1970s thousands of homes have been demolished, and since 2023 alone about 2,900 residential homes.

The problem starts with planning. The state tries to concentrate the Bedouin on as small an area as possible: a rural Jew in the northern Negev gets about 28 dunams from it, a rural Bedouin about half a dunam. A gap of 56 times.

The Council acts against the demolitions and against the policy that produces them, and proposes an alternative: The "Regional Councils Plan".

Legal advice service

The Council operates a legal advice service for residents of the unrecognized villages, led by an attorney. The service provides legal support and accompaniment on a range of issues, according to the residents' needs.

Community organizing

The unrecognized villages have no welfare system and no community organizers, so it is hard for residents to organize and demand what they are entitled to. The Council's community organizing project is meant to fill the gap.

Read more ↓

The community organizers help residents represent themselves, build capacity, cooperate and resolve disputes, and strengthen civic engagement and cohesion in the village.

The goal: strong, active communities that deal with their own challenges and lead change.

Changing public opinion

The Council's media project aims to change how Israeli society sees the Bedouin citizens of the Negev, the residents of the unrecognized villages and the question of recognizing them.

Read more ↓

The goal is to advance a change of policy: recognition of the villages, an end to demolitions and integration of the villages into a municipal framework, with a budget and services like any other community in Israel.

Mapping and planning the villages

Beersheba Valley: village structures colored by village. From the phase A report of the mapping project

The Council follows the government's plans for the villages and makes them accessible to the residents. So that planning will not be done over their heads, it initiated a comprehensive mapping project that begins in the Beersheba Valley: about 45,000 dunams between Beersheba and Nevatim, where about 39,000 people live in the unrecognized villages.

Read more ↓

The mapping documents not only geography and infrastructure, but also the social and cultural fabric of the villages, their needs and their potential.

The maps will serve the residents, researchers and planners, and will make it possible to plan the villages together with their residents, according to the way they actually live.

The October 7 war: emergency aid

On October 7, 2023, the residents of the unrecognized villages were left without the protection of the Iron Dome and without immediate medical response. Many of them worked in communities in the western Negev and were themselves harmed.

Read more ↓

In those days the Bedouin of the Negev showed resilience and solidarity: with no rescue forces, they went out to evacuate and rescue the wounded and reported to emergency centers.

The unrecognized villages have no local authority to look after the residents, so the Council stepped into the vacuum: it mapped the needs of the victims, helped with treatment of physical and mental injuries and with property damage claims, and worked with government ministries.

The Council distributed thousands of food packages adapted to Bedouin society and hundreds of first aid kits. At the same time it worked in the media to change how the Israeli public sees the Bedouin.
