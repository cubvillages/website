Why supporting the Council matters

The situation of the unrecognized villages is a painful reminder of the flaws in Israeli democracy. When the villages are recognized and their residents enjoy full civil equality, we will know that Israel as a whole is in better shape.

We are a small organization, and our tasks are many and complex:

  • Accompanying and assisting villages facing erasure
  • A regular presence at home and village demolition sites, documenting the demolitions and assisting residents
  • Daily response to lies and vicious incitement against the Bedouin community
  • Telling the story of the villages and their residents in the mass media and to some 30,000 followers on social media
  • Organizing demonstrations and public events
  • Ongoing advocacy in the Knesset, the government, planning committees and other bodies
  • Responding to urgent requests from village residents
  • Helping raise urgent funds for village struggles and residents in distress
  • Legal aid so village residents can exercise their rights
  • Developing and training local leadership

Since the Council for the Unrecognized Villages was founded in 1997, it has not received a single shekel from the state. All of its activity is funded by donations.

There is a solution, and it is on the table

The Regional Councils Plan: instead of more demolitions, recognized villages with their own councils, and a five-dunam family farm for every family. We are already working with several of the parties that may form the next government, and have persuaded some of them to adopt it. This is a decisive moment, but it is partial and at an early stage. Close accompaniment of the implementation, which will take years, is what will decide whether the plan actually happens
