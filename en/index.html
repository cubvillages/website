The voice of 150,000 citizens in 46 Bedouin villages in the Negev

The Council for the Unrecognized Bedouin Villages is a democratic organization whose representatives are elected by the village committees. We work to win recognition for the villages, stop home demolitions and make available the services every citizen is entitled to

Our vision

Recognized, developed villages, on the model of a workers' moshav. We have legal, safe homes, water, electricity, roads, good schools and health services. We make our living from our own land, from farming or any other business, and we preserve the Bedouin heritage, which is a national asset for all Israelis. We are full partners in democratic life and enjoy the state's resources, like every other citizen.

The price of the status quo

An unrecognized village is a village with no electricity, sewage, public transport, kindergartens or garbage collection, where every home is under threat of demolition. The residents pay the price, and so does the state: benefits instead of taxes, businesses that never open, social damage and an annual loss of tens of billions of shekels in output

Our goals

Recognition

Official state recognition of the Bedouin villages

Community-based planning

Plans that preserve the structure of the villages and the local tradition, with land for livelihoods

Local leadership

Leadership training and strengthening residents' capacities in education, welfare, employment and representation before the authorities

An end to discrimination

Accessible government services, a halt to home demolitions and an end to land expropriation

Public support

Working with opinion leaders and policymakers to broaden support for recognition of the villages

Solidarity

Uniting the Bedouin communities of the Negev for joint action to improve life in the villages

The proof

Al-Fuhari: the "Regional Councils Plan" has been working for 40 years

In the picturesque village south of Lehavim there is no poverty, no unemployment, no crime or violence, no school dropout and no home demolitions.

What does it have? A community that thrives economically, socially and educationally.

And why? Because of unique circumstances, the principles of the "Regional Councils Plan" were applied there 40 years ago.
